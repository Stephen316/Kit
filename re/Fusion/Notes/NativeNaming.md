### Saved study findings

Study scripts, reports and capture paths in this section are relative to [the Study directory](../Study/README.md).

`body-asm-identifier-decoding.json` replaces the main reader's ordinal body-root candidates with an observed identifier join. Each native mapping explicitly names its same-asset history blob; its stored handle matches the typed integer in an initial ASM BODY record. Complete bounded initial root tables, unique IDs, a bijective handle match and reciprocal BODY/lump links must agree before a root is attached. Seven focused saved files yield eighteen associations, with five independent API corroborations and five malformed guards. Two BoundaryFill bindings prove the earlier ordinal candidates wrong: stored handles 0 and 2 resolve to ASM records 3 and 1 respectively. Four diverse prior controls preserve all seventeen earlier associations. Four empty tables stay empty; an existing mixed-root Pipe layout remains unsupported.

The integrated reader discards ordinal candidates for every populated mapping, including rejected identifier joins. Only successful `asm_body_index` associations feed body placement. Earlier candidates remain diagnostic evidence in `previous_ordinal_candidate`; they cannot supply fallback geometry. Current visibility, final-body lifecycle, nonidentity transforms, component ownership and persistent face/edge identity are separate questions. The helper does not infer them from matching coordinates or numeric coincidences. `validate_body_asm_identifiers.py` reproduces the focused evidence.

`asm-int64-investigation.json` records an isolated token-framing investigation across 559 archives and 959 ASM blobs. An opaque-byte framing experiment reaches the final marker in all blobs. The production tokenizer is unchanged in this stage. A future contextual integer reader must validate the full observed schema, preserve unknown contexts and genuine non-finite-double rejection, and keep later-history entity semantics separate from framing completeness.
