# Select reciprocal Parasolid fin traversal per shell

The encoder can keep topology-use direction when face-sense reversal would collapse two distinct endpoints of a paired edge. That decision now follows each edge's two loop-to-face-to-shell ownership links. It applies to a closed shell only when every paired edge keeps opposite fin directions. A periodic edge whose endpoints share a vertex must also preserve opposite directions, and it cannot trigger the decision on its own. Open shells, cross-shell edges, unpaired edges and unproved direction pairs retain the prior face-sense traversal.

At PR revision `5605c58f5fdd7424bf192cf1480eae9959917e4d`, 19 of 25 receipt-pinned SOLIDWORKS discovery parts had a decoded solid. Each had one shell. The six sketch-only controls had no decoded solid. Among 58 pre-existing example parts, 26 had a decoded partition and one had multiple shells in one decoded carrier. This audit used committed archive identities and omitted the reserved profiles.

Its Config-0 partition has SHA-256 `bd5eaa3628f976a789edd0443cadb6f60377e512d71203caf1c7e34180972307`. The decoder gives its one carrier three closed solid shells with 4, 8 and 213 faces. Under the former face-sense rule, the third shell had 160 distinct-endpoint pairs with the same fin start. Using topology-use directions clears those 160. The other two shells had no distinct-endpoint conflicts, and no edge was shared between shells. Selecting traversal for the whole carrier nevertheless changed traversal on two faces of the first shell and four of the second. The new rule leaves those six face traversals in their established mode and changes the affected third shell.
